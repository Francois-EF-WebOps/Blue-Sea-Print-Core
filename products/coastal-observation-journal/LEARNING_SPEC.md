# Coastal Observation Journal — Learning Specification

**Version:** Learning-design pass 1  
**Status:** Design specification; learner testing and safety review remain outstanding.  
**Related standard:** [Learning Delivery Standard](../../../docs/LEARNING_DELIVERY_STANDARD.md)

## Learner and need
**Audience:** families, teachers, nature clubs and beginner coastal observers. Children use the worksheet with a responsible adult where supervision is needed.

**Need:** help beginners observe a coastal place carefully, record observations without guessing, and reflect on patterns while leaving the environment undisturbed.

**Prerequisite:** ability to notice and describe visible features, with support as needed. Species-identification knowledge is not required.

## Learning outcomes
After completing the journal, the learner should be able to:
1. **Describe** at least two visible coastal observations using sensory or positional details without inventing an identification.
2. **Record** a change or similarity over time using a simple written, drawn or dictated note.
3. **Distinguish** an observation from an interpretation or unanswered question.
4. **Identify** at least one way to observe responsibly without disturbing the place or putting themselves at risk.

## Existing three-page sequence

| Page | Learning purpose | Learner action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 — Arrive & Notice | Establish safe habits and focus attention | Check the setting; note date, general place, weather noticed and duration | Context fields and pre-observation checks |
| 02 — Record | Practise careful description and noticing change | Record visible details, compare changes, draw the view, write a question | Two or more descriptions, a sketch and one question |
| 03 — Reflect | Separate confident observations from uncertainty | Identify details, uncertainty, similarities/differences and responsible actions | Reflection entries and care checklist |

## Proposed HTML improvements
These are design recommendations, not yet implemented in the worksheet.

### Page 01 — Arrive & Notice
- Add the objective: “I can notice and describe what I see without disturbing it.”
- Add an observation-versus-guess example: “A small bird is standing near the water” (observation) versus “It is looking for food” (interpretation unless supported by evidence).
- Keep the stop rule prominent.

### Page 02 — Record
- Add: “What do you see? What colour, shape, movement or position can you describe?”
- Clarify the confidence column as: “Did I see this, infer it, or am I unsure?”
- Add an optional repeat-look prompt: “What is different? What stayed the same?”
- Preserve generous drawing space.

### Page 03 — Reflect
- Add a self-check: “I described it / recorded a change / named something I am unsure about / left the place undisturbed.”
- Add optional transfer: “What could you observe from the same safe place on another day?”
- Do not grade learners on knowing species names. Careful description and honest uncertainty are the intended skills.

## Differentiation
**Support:** adult/peer reads prompts aloud; learner may draw, point, dictate or use short phrases. Offer a small word bank such as smooth, rough (only if visible from a safe distance), bright, dull, moving, still, near, far, larger and smaller. Complete one row together.

**Core:** record at least two observations, a sketch and one question, then complete the reflection.

**Extend:** return to the same safe observation point on another day and compare records. Separate repeated observations from possible explanations; state what additional evidence would be needed.

## Feedback guidance
- If the learner guesses a species or cause, ask: “What did you actually notice that makes you think that?”
- Treat “I don't know” as a useful question; ask what could be checked later from a trusted source.
- Accept “nothing changed that I could notice” as a valid observation.
- Never encourage closer approach, touching, collection or entering an unsafe area to improve an observation.

## Formative assessment

| Outcome | Meets the intended outcome when… |
|---|---|
| Describe | Gives two specific visible details without unsupported identification |
| Record | Captures a visible detail and a change/similarity, or accurately records no noticeable change |
| Distinguish | Labels at least one item as uncertain or as a question rather than presenting a guess as fact |
| Responsible observation | Identifies and follows the non-disturbance and stop rules |

## Safety and scope
This is an observation worksheet, not a survival, foraging, fishing, swimming, medical, weather, tide, navigation or emergency guide. Observe only from a lawful, stable place above the waterline; follow local rules and warnings; stop when conditions or footing are unsafe. Children remain with a responsible adult when supervision is needed.

No field testing, local expert safety review or formal educational validation has been completed. These limitations must remain visible until addressed.

## Pilot questions
When a suitable educator/family pilot is available, check:
- Can learners distinguish what they saw from what they guessed?
- Can they complete the record without an adult rewriting their observations?
- Are writing/drawing spaces and instructions usable at intended print size?
- Do learners understand the stop and non-disturbance rules?
- Which prompts cause confusion or unnecessary effort?

Record actual observations and revisions. Do not claim learning gains from informal feedback alone.
