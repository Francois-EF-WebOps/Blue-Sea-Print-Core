# Learning Delivery Standard

**Status:** Proposed reusable production standard  
**Applies to:** Blue-Sea-Print-Core learning products, printables, digital activities, guides and multimedia lessons.

## Product promise
Every learning product must help a defined learner make a demonstrable change in knowledge, skill, judgement or ability. Completion, attractive artwork and character recognition are not proof of learning.

## Required design brief
Record the audience, prior knowledge, language and accessibility needs; the real learner/educator/buyer need; 1–3 observable learning outcomes; evidence for each outcome; device, print, time, connectivity and supervision constraints; and explicit safety/scope boundaries.

Do not infer market demand from educational usefulness alone. Validate demand with a real buyer, educator, learner group or distribution partner before investing in a larger product line.

## Instructional sequence
Adapt this sequence to the subject; do not force every product into identical page counts.

1. **Connect:** establish relevance with a question, situation, observation or story.
2. **Explain:** present one manageable idea at a time in plain language.
3. **Model:** show what a good attempt looks like; make the thinking visible.
4. **Guide:** provide a supported first attempt, prompts, hints or worked examples.
5. **Practise:** let the learner make decisions or produce something, with useful feedback.
6. **Apply:** reduce support and use the idea in a fresh context.
7. **Reflect and retrieve:** recall, explain, compare or identify uncertainty.
8. **Check:** collect evidence against the outcomes and record what needs revision.

Short products may combine stages, but must include meaningful learner action and evidence.

## Presentation rules
- One primary learning purpose per page, screen or activity.
- Use headings that tell learners what to do or understand.
- Prefer short, concrete instructions; define necessary technical terms.
- Pair text and visuals when the visual carries information. Decorative images must not compete with instructions.
- Use accurate, respectful examples appropriate to the learner's context.
- Provide sufficient writing, drawing, selection or interaction space.
- Keep navigation and recurring elements predictable.
- Characters such as Fin and Nik should model, question, guide or demonstrate—not merely decorate.
- Do not imply mastery because a learner completed a page or received praise.

## Differentiation
Offer optional entry points and extensions rather than assuming all learners of one age have the same ability.

- **Support:** vocabulary bank, picture cues, sentence starters, worked examples, fewer choices, adult/peer discussion.
- **Core:** the intended independent task.
- **Extend:** explain reasoning, compare evidence, transfer to a new situation or create a new example.

Age bands inform language and format, but prior knowledge, reading fluency, language proficiency, disability and context matter too.

## Technical delivery
Choose the simplest format that meets the learning need.

- **Print:** reliable page size, readable type, adequate contrast, margins and writing space; verify PDF page breaks.
- **Mobile/web:** responsive layout, touch-friendly controls, legible text, clear focus/error states and no unnecessary sign-in.
- **Offline/low bandwidth:** provide downloadable or printable alternatives when practical.
- **Accessibility:** semantic structure, logical reading order, keyboard support for interactive content, meaningful alt text, non-colour-only cues and captions/transcripts where relevant.
- **Privacy:** collect no learner personal data unless necessary, justified and disclosed; never expose children's information in public repositories or analytics.
- **Dependencies/cost:** disclose external services, account requirements, API requirements and recurring costs. Core use should not silently depend on paid services.
- **Portability:** preserve editable sources and document how to produce learner-facing outputs.

## Assessment and evidence
For every outcome, define an observable check and what a successful response looks like. Prefer authentic tasks over recognition-only quizzes when the intended outcome is application. Record common errors, feedback/next steps and what has or has not been tested with actual learners.

Do not claim improved learning, retention or educational effectiveness without appropriate evidence. Separate design intent, desk review, usability testing and measured outcomes.

## Quality gates
Before release, record the relevant checks:
1. Learning review: outcomes, activities and evidence align.
2. Content review: factual accuracy, clarity, age/context suitability and attribution.
3. Safety review: hazards and limitations are explicit; specialist review obtained where needed.
4. Presentation review: hierarchy, readability, visual consistency and character continuity.
5. Technical review: print/device rendering, accessibility basics, offline behaviour and broken links.
6. Rights review: ownership/licences for text, images, fonts, music, models and third-party material.
7. User review: representative learner/educator testing where feasible; findings recorded.
8. Release review: scope, version, price/licence, support and limitations approved.

Mark non-applicable checks with a reason. Untested items must be labelled untested, not passed.

## Improvement loop
After a pilot, collect only feedback needed to improve the product. Look for learner hesitation/misunderstanding, independent explanation/application, usability on real devices/printers and educator/buyer willingness to use, recommend or pay. Prioritise by learner impact and severity. Keep a short change log and re-run affected checks.

## Required product records
Each product should maintain a learning specification, editable source/build instructions, QA/release checklist, rights/provenance records, and pilot feedback/change log.

This is a production baseline, not a claim of accreditation or compliance with every jurisdiction's education or accessibility requirements.
